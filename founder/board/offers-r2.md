# Offers lens (round 2 — 1000 users)

Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong

- **The market exists and is easy to name.** Anime/manga/light-novel readers are a real, large, growing audience. On the lens's first market test (is there a crowd with this pain), this clears.
- **The product covers the whole path to the outcome.** Read → save progress → discuss → rate → progress in ranks. That is a genuine value stack, not a single feature: each problem a reader hits between "I want to read" and "I want to talk about it" has a solution bolted on. Most free side projects have one layer; this has five.
- **Low effort and low time-to-value for the reader.** Vertical-scroll reading, saved progress, and a chat that loads = the buyer is asked for almost nothing. The effort/sacrifice half of the value equation is the strongest part of this business.
- **Moderation tooling at 1000 users is a differentiator, not a nice-to-have.** Roles, bans, appeals, reports, audit logs — this is the layer free reading sites usually lack, and it is what makes a community survivable past a few hundred people.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The core reading content is copyright-soft and the scale just changed.** Hosting a pre-processed Death Note PDF for ~10 friends is invisible; hosting it for ~1000 readers is a different legal and hosting posture. A single takedown or host suspension removes the dream outcome entirely — the value equation collapses to "a chat room." This is an open question with no number attached yet; it needs an answer before any offer is built, and /founder-competitors is the right place to check how comparable sites at this size survive (and what they pay for it).
2. **There is no offer.** Price is $0, there is no monetization path, and the lens's core test — "could you double the price and still be the obvious choice?" — cannot be run because there is no price. Free-to-user with no revenue means the value equation has never been tested: nobody has demonstrated that these 1000 readers value the dream outcome enough to part with money. The lens's second market test (do the buyers have money to spend?) is unproven for this specific crowd; whether this audience will pay anything at all is an open question for /founder-consumer.
3. **No stated acquisition channel, so the market's fourth test is blank.** "Easy to find and reach" is unanswerable: nobody knows how these 1000 arrived, whether that was a one-time accident, or what the next 1000 cost. If the channel was luck, the market score drops from "large" to "unreachable" — and a great offer in an unreachable market is the lens's most common failure. Cost of the next user is an open question for /founder-consumer.

Also unresolved, but subordinate: **infrastructure cost at 1000 users is unpriced**, which is an open question for /founder-cfo. It does not kill the offer, but until it is priced, nobody can say whether any future price covers delivery.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. How did the 1000 users arrive, and what did it cost you in money or time?
2. If you charged ₹100/month tomorrow, what percentage of the 1000 would stay — and what would they say they are paying for?
3. What is the one dream outcome you are selling: uninterrupted reading, or a community that talks about what it reads? Which one do people come back for?
4. How are you hosting Death Note and ORV right now, and what happens to the product if that content is removed?
5. What does delivery cost per month at 1000 users, all in?
6. Is it still invite-only? If not, what stops the next 1000 from arriving the same way the first did?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

Vote is PASS, so there is no FUND IF. Recorded as the conditions that would flip this to FUND IF on a future round:

1. Acquisition channel identified and repeatable, with a cost-per-user number from /founder-consumer.
2. Infrastructure cost priced monthly, all-in, by /founder-cfo — and a stated plan for who pays it.
3. At least one paid tier or revenue experiment run against the existing 1000 users, with a real conversion number from /founder-consumer.
4. A defensible answer on the copyright exposure: what content is hosted, on what basis, and what the product looks like if it is removed.
