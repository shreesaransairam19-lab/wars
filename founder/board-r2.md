# The board — WARS (round 2 — 1000 users)

**Vote: 2 PASS, 1 FUND IF. Average score: 3.3/10** (Offers 3 PASS, Monopoly 3 PASS, Product 4 FUND IF)

Round 1 (10 friends, $0, invite-only) was 3× FUND IF at 5.3. The audience change alone moved the board to a near- PASS: the product work is still real, but the business around it now has three un-answers where round 1 had three solved problems.

## What changed, in the board's words

- Round 1's strongest point — "distribution is solved: the founder invites his friends" — is gone. At 1000 strangers the site competes head-to-head with MangaDex, aggregators, and official apps, and **no acquisition channel has been named**.
- Round 1's #1 risk — free-tier caps — is no longer a risk to manage; it is an invalid plan. Supabase Free / Vercel Hobby cannot serve 1000 users. **Paid infra is required and nobody has priced it.** Cost now scales with every new user while revenue stays $0: diseconomy of scale.
- Copyright exposure moved from "accepted private risk" to existential: 1000 discoverable readers of pre-processed Death Note/ORV is a takedown or host suspension away from zero, with no cash reserve behind it.
- Moderation (roles, bans, appeals, reports, audit logs) flipped from scope-bloat (round 1, Product lens) to load-bearing: at 1000 cold strangers it is the product's survival layer — and it is unproven, run by a solo founder.

## Risks raised by more than one member

1. **No named acquisition channel** (all three).
2. **Copyright at community scale** (all three; Offers called it the collapsed value equation, Monopoly called it the one risk that ends the business).
3. **Unpriced infrastructure vs. $0 revenue** (all three).
4. Moderation/trust hardening unproven at stranger-scale (Offers noted it as differentiator; Product called it the top kill risk).

## Conditions to re-submit (merged)

- [ ] Name and prove **one acquisition channel** with a real per-user number (`/founder-consumer`).
- [ ] **Price the infrastructure** monthly at 1000 users (and at 5000) — and say who pays it (`/founder-cfo`). The WARS_PLAN.md Key Decisions table (Supabase Free, Vercel Hobby) is now false and must be rewritten.
- [ ] **Settle the content posture:** what is hosted, on what basis, what survives a takedown. Decide before the takedown.
- [ ] Prove **moderation works cold**: reports, bans, appeals tested against abuse before opening the doors.
- [ ] Pick **the one thing** for a cold arrival (reading *or* community) and cut the rest to a slice.
- [ ] Name the **small market** to own first (a fandom, a language, a community) — "1000 anime readers" is a slice of a big market nobody leads.

## The strongest version the board can see

Two honest paths exist. **Path A (keep the round-1 shape):** private/invite-only, small, $0 or near-$0, brilliant readers + thin chat — the round-1 board funded this 3–0; 1000 users is simply not the goal. **Path B (the 1000-user shape):** a real community business — which requires, before any more feature work: a named niche and channel, priced infrastructure, a content posture, a revenue experiment (donations/tier), and hardened moderation. Building the round-1 product for the Path-B audience — which is what the current plan does — is the one combination both PASS votes reject.

## Disagreement to preserve

The Product lens was the lone FUND IF, still betting the scope can be cut to a working slice with trust surfaces first. The Offers and Monopoly lenses hold that no slice is fundable while channel, cost, and content posture are blank. The chair notes the Product member's path (cut to one thing, prove moderation) is compatible with both PASS members' re-submission conditions — do that work and re-convene.
